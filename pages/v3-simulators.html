Any value can be set once, or held until it is released, and the client reads exactly that value. image: /assets/img/v3/screens/simulators/meter-client.webp image_alt: Nova Vector Simulators console of a three-phase energy meter with the voltage of phase 1 held at 253 V, and a command-line Modbus client reading 253 from the same register caption: A simulated energy meter with one voltage held at 253 V, and a standard Modbus client reading exactly that value. - eyebrow: Systems, not separate devices heading: Devices that share one model of the plant paragraphs: - Each simulator system couples its devices through one model, so they behave causally. When the HVAC unit of a battery enclosure fails, the enclosure heats up, the BMS reports over-temperature, and the inverter derates. When one machine on a production line breaks down, the machines after it starve and the ones before it block. - Random values on independent devices cannot tell that story. Cross-device alarms, data-quality handling, and work-order features need devices that react to each other the way real equipment does. image: /assets/img/v3/screens/simulators/system-devices.webp image_alt: Nova Vector Simulators console of a battery storage enclosure with its BMS, temperature and humidity sensors, enclosure controller, HVAC unit, inverter, network switch, and UPS, all running caption: A battery storage enclosure with ten devices, from the BMS and inverter to the HVAC unit and UPS, driven by one model. - eyebrow: Real protocols heading: On the wire, where the field equipment would be paragraphs: - The simulators speak the protocols your system already reads. Any client that speaks them can connect, whether a SCADA, a building management system, a historian, an MQTT broker, or an IoT platform. None of them needs Nova Vector. - Device maps are plain configuration files. The HVAC failure scenario on the battery enclosure, for example, checks fifteen outcomes across BACnet, Modbus, and SNMP, and it runs the same way in the lab, in a customer meeting, and in continuous integration. - Every reference system runs an endless scenario on the wall clock, with shifts, breaks, product changes, charge and discharge cycles, and random stops, so dashboards and reports always have data. For a short demonstration or a test run, the model clock runs from 0.1 to 600 times real time, and can be paused and stepped. image: /assets/img/v3/screens/simulators/scenarios.webp image_alt: Nova Vector Simulators system page of a battery storage enclosure, with the model clock, the rate, and the scenarios, from communication loss and grid outage to HVAC failure and network impairment caption: The scenarios of the battery enclosure, from an endless day of operation to an HVAC failure, a grid outage, and a network partition. - eyebrow: Faults and data quality heading: Break things on purpose, before they break in the field paragraphs: - Any device or signal can be taken offline, slowed down, made to drop requests or answer with an error code, frozen, made noisy or drifting, or given uncertain or bad quality, by hand from the console or from a scenario. Each fault ends on its own after the time you set. - The client sees what it would see in the field. A Modbus client's reads time out when the device answers too late, a gateway answers with exception 0x0B when its device is offline, and a value of bad quality fails every read that includes it with a device failure. An offline SNMP device stops sending traps, and an offline MQTT node disconnects. - Network impairment adds latency, jitter, packet loss, or a complete partition between the simulators and your system. The client's connections time out while the link is impaired and its reads succeed again when the link is back, so you see how alarms, trends, and data-quality handling behave when the connection is poor, and how they recover. image: /assets/img/v3/screens/simulators/faults.webp image_alt: Nova Vector Simulators fault page with a stale sensor, a humidity reading of uncertain quality, and an offline UPS, each with its duration caption: Three active faults on a battery enclosure, a stale sensor, a humidity reading of uncertain quality, and an offline UPS. - eyebrow: Your own models heading: Bring your engineering model to the protocols operators see paragraphs: - A battery, process, or equipment model built in Simulink, Modelica, or another modelling tool can drive the simulated devices. Exported as an FMU, in FMI 2.0 or 3.0 co-simulation, it replaces that part of the physics, and its results appear in the registers, objects, and telemetry your system reads. - The modeller exports the model once. To add it to a system, a technician copies two files and edits one entry. Model parameters can be changed from the console or from a scenario, for example to show how an aged battery pack runs hotter. A modelling tool licence is needed to export the model, not to run it. image: /assets/img/v3/screens/simulators/external-model.webp image_alt: Nova Vector Simulators external model card with a battery FMU in FMI 3.0, its step cost, the signals it reads and drives, and its parameters caption: A battery model in FMI 3.0 drives the BMS of the simulated enclosure, with its inputs, outputs, and parameters on one card. - eyebrow: Scale heading: Find out where the system breaks before the roll-out does paragraphs: - MQTT fleets simulate hundreds or thousands of edge nodes, each with its own login and topic.
